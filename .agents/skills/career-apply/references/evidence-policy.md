# Evidence and writing policy

- Use only canonical `data/experience/*.yaml` and `data/profile/*.yaml` for candidate history. JD language and organization research describe the vacancy, not the candidate. Synthetic examples demonstrate the format but cannot prove real experience.
- Classify every meaningful requirement: **direct** (explicitly proved by canonical facts), **transferable** (adjacent capability with its original context clear), or **gap** (not supported). Never turn proximity into ownership, familiarity into practice, or a planned learning topic into work experience.
- Each bullet needs at least one relevant `source_fact_ids` entry. Preserve canonical titles and dates; preserve exact metric values, units, and qualifiers. A number in rewritten text must be present in a referenced fact's `metrics`. Check summary and project names manually too: validation cannot prove every free-text claim.
- Write concrete challenge → decision/action → result where supported. Prefer outcome before tools, but do not imply a measured outcome where none exists. Keep independent projects and publications separate from employment. Select evidence for both JD fit and supported differentiators; omit repetition rather than strong facts solely because the JD lacks the keyword.
- The honest variant can use direct evidence and carefully bounded transferable wording. An optional stretched variant may change positioning but must not add unverified skills, certifications, domains, or study claims. Record any permitted difference, its exact canonical fact IDs, and wording boundary in a separate review note. Proposed learning stays outside both resumes.
- Maintain a gap note with `GAPS` (unsupported), `TRANSFERABLE` (bounded language + IDs), and `STRENGTHS` (direct support + IDs). Disclose material gaps to the user.
